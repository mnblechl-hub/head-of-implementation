This is an independent operating-model concept created by Megan Blechl for a Head of Implementation role. Customer names, people, dates, and deployment data are fictional.

01 · THE OPERATING MODEL

Seven gates. One controlled path to value.

A deployment moves only when the outcome is clear, ownership is named, and evidence is recorded.

  1. 01

    Scope confirmed

    Sites, workflows, integrations, responsibilities, and exclusions agreed.

    Evidence: signed scope
  2. 02

    Environment ready

    Security, hosting, network, identity, and test environments approved.

    Evidence: readiness sign-off
  3. 03

    Solution configured

    Roles, permissions, templates, and site settings validated.

    Evidence: configuration record
  4. 04

    Integration validated

    EHR, identity, mappings, and clinical scenarios tested end to end.

    Evidence: test results
  5. 05

    Go-live ready

    Governance, enablement, support, cutover, and rollback confirmed.

    Evidence: go-live approval
  6. 06

    Stabilised

    Production is stable, critical defects are closed, and monitoring is active.

    Evidence: stability measures
  7. 07

    Handover accepted

    Customer Success and Support accept ownership, risks, and actions.

    Evidence: accepted handover

02 · WORKED CASE STUDY

Turn a technical blocker into an executive decision.

Daisy Health is approaching cutover without an accountable customer identity owner.

01 · SIGNAL

Ownership is missing

The SAML attribute map cannot be approved because no customer IT owner is accountable.

02 · CONSEQUENCE

The buffer reaches zero

If ownership is not assigned within 48 hours, the team cannot protect a stable production cutover.

03 · DECISION

Assign or replan

The CIO assigns an identity owner today, or cutover moves one week. The risk does not remain implicit.

04 · EVIDENCE

Pass the gate deliberately

Proceed only when the attribute map, test result, owner, and sign-off are recorded.

03 · LEADERSHIP JUDGMENT

Protect the customer and the roadmap.

Two common implementation pressures require explicit trade-offs—not status reporting.

CAPACITY COLLISION

Two cutovers need the same integration engineer.

Choose a response, then see the delivery consequence.

Select an option to expose the trade-off.

SCOPE BOUNDARY

Configure the interface. Do not create a product gap.

Iris Health’s ADT variance matches an existing mapping pattern. Configuration resolves the requirement in four days; a new build takes twelve and consumes roadmap capacity.

Configure 4 daysNew build 12 days
Decision: use the proven configuration path.
